# Teacher's Proven Activities

This is the tutor's own list of activities that have worked well in practice. Prefer these over `activity-bank.md` whenever one fits the lesson — treat `activity-bank.md` as the fallback, not the other way around.

When picking "randomly," don't pick uniformly at random from the whole list — filter first to activities that fit the level, the time available, and the lesson's language/skill focus, then choose among those. When a specific activity is requested by name, use it as-is and adapt only the topic/content/scenario to the student.

## Vocabulary & input
- **Fresh Vocabulary related to interests – focus on collocations**: the core vocab-presentation activity; see the Fresh Vocabulary section rule in SKILL.md — always collocation-first, tied to the student's interests
- **Read a (generated) dialogue using new vocabulary**: write a short dialogue (2 speakers, 8-15 lines) embedding the lesson's new vocab/grammar naturally in context; good comprehension check before production
- **Read a found article – discuss vocab, topic**: use or reference a real article on the student's interest area; pre-teach blocking vocab, discuss content and any new vocab encountered. Suits B1+ where authentic text is manageable
- **Describe a picture**: student describes an image in detail (or compares two images); good low-prep speaking warm-up or vocab-activation task, scales from A2 (simple description) to C1 (inference, speculation about context)
- **Use a very short media clip, discuss message and opinions**: short video/audio clip (1-3 min) as a prompt for listening + opinion discussion; keep clip length short relative to discussion time
- **Use data or charts to discuss trends and solutions**: present a simple chart/graph; practice describing trends (rise/fall/plateau language) then discuss causes/solutions. Good for B1+ and business-English contexts

## Speaking & discussion
- **Speaking Practice – Discussion topics and questions**: a set of open discussion questions on the lesson topic, ordered from concrete to abstract/opinion-based
- **Sentence upgrades**: give a plain/basic sentence, have the student rewrite it three ways:
  - Using new vocab from this lesson
  - Changing the tone (funny, rude, formal, etc.)
  - Shifting between indirect and direct language (e.g. "Give me that" → "Would you mind passing that?")
- **Role Play**: assign roles and a scenario tied to the student's interest/goal (e.g. a workplace negotiation for a business student); give brief role cards, not full scripts
- **Ranking scenario (4-5 items)**: give 4-5 items/options related to the topic; student ranks them by a criterion and justifies the order — strong for comparative language and opinion-giving
- **Impossible choice scenario**: present a forced dilemma with no clearly "correct" answer; student must choose and defend it — good for hedging language, opinion structures
- **Mini Debates**: short (5-10 min) debate on a topic connected to the lesson; assign or let student pick a side
- **Devil's Advocate – argue for a different position**: after the student states an honest opinion, have them argue the opposite position; builds argumentation language and flexibility, best for B2+
- **Inbox activity – respond to a series of messages (verbally or in writing)**: present 3-5 short messages (emails/texts) needing replies; student responds to each, either aloud or in writing — good for register-switching practice
- **Press conference – respond to journalist questions**: student takes on a role (public figure, expert, someone from a scenario) and fields a rapid series of questions; strong for fluency under mild pressure, works well for exam-interview prep

## Roleplay/scenario formats needing setup detail
- **Gallery tour – describe a series of artworks and respond to questions**: present 3-4 images (art, or interest-relevant visuals); student "guides a tour," describing each and fielding questions — good extended-speaking task
- **Respond to Subway takes with a randomly selected tone**: present a short opinionated "hot take" style statement (in the vein of social-media "unpopular opinion" posts); student responds, using a tone assigned at random (e.g. diplomatic, sarcastic, enthusiastic) — good register/tone-flexibility practice, best B1+
- **Hometown Hero local newspaper interview**: student plays someone being interviewed as a "local hero" for a hometown newspaper; interviewer (tutor or peer) asks human-interest questions — combines narrative past tenses with personalization if tied to the student's actual background

## Fit notes
- Debate/Devil's Advocate/Press conference need enough language for argumentation — reserve for B1+ (ideally B2+)
- Describe a picture, Ranking scenario, and Role Play scale down cleanly to A2 with simpler scenarios/vocab
- Anything needing an authentic article or media clip depends on finding/generating age- and level-appropriate material — don't default to complex native-speed content for A1-A2
